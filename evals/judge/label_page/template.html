Grader calibration labels

The memo grader is an AI model. It is trusted only as far as it agrees with you, so label each of these 30 items as you judge it. The claims come from real drafts of synthetic borrower memos; some have been altered.

Pick Not sure rather than guess, and add a note when an item is borderline. Your choices save as you go, so you can stop and come back.

How to judge

These are the same rules the AI grader is given, so your labels and its verdicts answer the same question.

Citation items: are the source lines enough?

Read the claim, then only the grey source lines under it. Ignore anything you know from elsewhere.

Supported every number and every statement in the claim is in those lines, or follows directly from them. A number written without commas, or rounded (13.8 million for 13,801,200), still counts. A ratio the claim says was "computed by the platform's tools" counts if the figures it rests on are among the lines; you don't need to recompute it.

Not supported any part is missing, wrong or needs another source: a number that differs, a row that isn't the one the claim talks about, or a statement the lines can't show, such as "no risk flags were raised" or "revenue grew every year".

Example: "FY2025 revenue was 13801200" citing the revenue row that ends 13,801,200 is supported. The same claim citing the EBITDA row is not.

Finding items: does the memo say it?

Read the finding, then open the memo below it.

Covered the memo states the finding's substance, including its key number where it has one. Different wording, rounding, or leaving out the policy reference (CP-4.1) is fine.

Not covered the memo doesn't state it, states only part of it, or says something that contradicts it.

Example: for "Pro forma DSCR of 0.93 is below the policy minimum of 1.25", a memo saying "pro forma DSCR 0.93 is below the 1.25 minimum" covers it. A memo that only says "DSCR is weak" does not.
